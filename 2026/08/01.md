#### 2. German court rules Suno infringed copyrights and orders disclosure of revenue

- **Source:** [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/german-court-rules-that-ai-music-firm-suno-violated-copyrights/a-78152227)
- **Published:** 2026-07-31 (updated)
- **Why it matters:** The ruling is an important early test of whether generative-music companies can be held liable when model outputs closely reproduce protected works, with potential consequences for licensing and creator compensation.

The Munich Regional Court ruled that US-based AI music company Suno violated copyrights, ordered it to disclose illicit revenue and said it must pay damages whose amount has not yet been set. German collecting society GEMA demonstrated outputs resembling protected songs including “Forever Young,” “Mambo No. 5” and “Daddy Cool”; Suno acknowledged that the original songs at issue were among material used to train its model, but disagreed with the ruling and is considering an appeal. The decision is from a lower court and may not be final.

#### 3. Google withdraws one-click AI satellite-image tool after misuse concerns

- **Source:** [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/31/nx-s1-5914652/google-adds-ai-to-satellite-images-raising-fears-of-deepfakes-in-the-sky)
- **Published:** 2026-07-31 (updated)
- **Why it matters:** Making synthetic satellite scenes inside a widely trusted verification platform could accelerate disinformation and make genuine evidence easier to dismiss.

Google said it was rolling back a Google Earth feature that let users generate satellite-style images from text prompts, one day after launch, after screenshots appeared to violate its policies. NPR said it could generate false scenes of Iran's Kharg Island on fire and flooding around the US Capitol, while an open-source researcher reported that prompts involving conflict and disaster were not refused. Google said generated images carried SynthID watermarks and that it would work on stronger guardrails; those controls did not prevent the cited deceptive-looking outputs.

#### 1. Malaysia's hybrid-work policy for eligible civil servants takes effect

- **Source:** [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/08/01/civil-servants-swap-desks-for-hybrid-rhythm-as-spotme-tracks-malaysias-new-workday-experiment/229839)
- **Published:** 2026-08-01
- **Why it matters:** Making hybrid work a permanent arrangement for eligible officers changes how parts of Malaysia's public service operate while preserving departmental control and in-person requirements for essential services.

The Hybrid Workday policy took effect on August 1, allowing eligible civil servants to work three days in the office and two days from home or another approved location. Heads of department determine eligibility and may require officers to report in person, while essential services including security, defence, enforcement, education, health, courts and counter services are excluded. Attendance and output will be monitored through the SPOT-Me system, and officers must remain contactable and comply with official-secrecy requirements.
