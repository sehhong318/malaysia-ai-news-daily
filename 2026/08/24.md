#### 1. Cabinet approves six immediate security measures for KLIA

- **Published:** 2026-08-24
- **Why it matters:** The package moves airport-security reform from review to an approved pilot that can later extend to Malaysia's other international gateways.

#### 2. Median pay for workers aged 25–29 remains below its pre-pandemic level

- **Source:** [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/wages-lag-for-malaysias-young-workers/)
- **Published:** 2026-08-24
- **Why it matters:** Persistently weak early-career pay can compound into lower lifetime earnings, delayed home ownership and a wider intergenerational wealth gap.

Department of Statistics Malaysia figures cited in the report put the age group's 2024 median monthly salary at RM2,095, 5% below RM2,206 in 2019; it was the only age band still below its pre-pandemic level. Bank Negara Malaysia has separately highlighted wage compression and high-skilled underemployment of about 35%–36%, while government responses include the RM1,700 minimum wage and Progressive Wage Policy. The figures describe medians and broad labour-market conditions rather than every young worker's experience.

- **Why it matters:** Malaysia's move into chip design and higher-value AI work depends on converting its long industrial history into enough skilled graduates, technicians and returning experts.

He argued that more complex, better-paid work and domestic intellectual property are needed to retain and attract talent; the workforce target remains an ambition rather than a completed outcome.

- **Published:** 2026-08-24

Malaysian SMEs across food, manufacturing, logistics and retail are increasingly able to buy specialised Chinese equipment directly as digital procurement and logistics improve. Businesses interviewed said quality has narrowed the gap with established suppliers, but they now assess total ownership cost, including warranties, repairs, spare parts and local technical support, rather than headline price alone. The report describes a broad procurement shift based on company and industry interviews, not a quantified national market-share estimate.

#### 5. Indonesia challenges Malaysia's palm-oil pricing benchmark with a new commodity exchange

- **Source:** [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/week-asia/economics/article/3364890/indonesia-takes-malaysia-battle-over-palm-oil-pricing)
- **Published:** 2026-08-24
- **Why it matters:** If Jakarta can shift price discovery from Bursa Malaysia, it could alter hedging, tax collection and regional influence across the world's largest palm-oil trade.

Indonesia is advancing its Strategic Mineral and Commodity Exchange as part of President Prabowo Subianto's effort to make the country a price setter for palm oil and eventually nickel and coal, with the Financial Services Authority appointing exchange oversight on August 19. Analysts cautioned that control of physical supply does not automatically displace Bursa Malaysia's crude-palm-oil futures contract, which remains the industry's main pricing and hedging reference. The impact on Bursa Malaysia is therefore a competitive scenario, not an accomplished transfer of pricing power.

- **Published:** 2026-08-24
- **Why it matters:** A measurable reuse saving shows how circular-economy practices can reduce landfill waste while also improving the economics of municipal services.

Alam Flora said recovering and remanufacturing damaged waste bins saves an estimated RM2.4 million a year, alongside wider recycling, composting and traceability work under its Waste-to-Value programme. #### 7. Analysis projects Malaysia's pension bill at RM120 billion by 2040

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/815376)
- **Published:** 2026-08-24
- **Why it matters:** Rapidly rising retirement obligations can crowd out development spending long after the public-sector hiring decisions that created them.

A *The Edge Malaysia* analysis says pension payments rose to RM38.6 billion, or 11.5% of government revenue, in 2025 and are estimated at RM42.8 billion this year, while the government's current-trajectory projection reaches RM120 billion by 2040. It links the pressure to civil-service expansion from 676,000 workers in 2000 to 1.27 million in 2018, rising pay and longevity, with nearly one million pension beneficiaries today. This is an analytical interpretation of government and workforce data; the 2040 amount is a projection, not a realised liability payment.
