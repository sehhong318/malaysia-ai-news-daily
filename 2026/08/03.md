#### 1. Malaysia's climate-adaptation financing needs estimated at nearly RM3.5 trillion by 2050

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia, reporting remarks by the Securities Commission chairman](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/813036)
- **Published:** 2026-08-03
- **Why it matters:** The estimate shows the exceptional scale of capital Malaysia may need to protect communities and infrastructure from climate impacts, and the likely need for public-private financing.

Securities Commission chairman Mohammad Faiz Azmi said Malaysia's climate-adaptation financing needs are estimated at nearly RM3.5 trillion by 2050, citing the World Bank's 2026 Malaysia Country Climate and Development Report. He said adaptation projects will probably require blended-finance structures in which public-sector participation, patient capital or credit enhancements reduce risk before private investment follows. The figure is an estimate cited at a July 30 showcase, not committed government spending.

#### 2. Malaysia considers conditional easing of raw rare-earth export restrictions

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia, carrying Bloomberg reporting](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/813030)
- **Published:** 2026-08-03
- **Why it matters:** Any relaxation could alter Malaysia's strategy for capturing more value from critical minerals while affecting global efforts to diversify rare-earth supply beyond China.

Deputy Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability Minister Syed Ibrahim Syed Noh said authorities are assessing whether to allow some exports of unprocessed rare earths after imposing a moratorium in 2024 to encourage domestic processing. He said any exports would carry conditions tied to inbound investment, technology transfer and overseas research and development, but gave no timeline for a regulatory change. Malaysia has not announced that the restrictions have been lifted.

#### 1. Minnesota's ban on AI “nudification” services takes effect despite xAI challenge

- **Source:** [CBS News Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-ban-ai-nudification-tech-in-effect-obstacles/)
- **Published:** 2026-08-02
- **Why it matters:** The first-in-the-US measure could become a model for regulating services that create non-consensual sexual images, while the pending constitutional challenge will test its legal limits.

Minnesota's ban on AI services that turn real photographs into sexualised or pornographic images took effect on August 1 after a federal judge declined xAI's late request to stop the law before its effective date. CBS News reported that platform operators can face penalties of up to US$500,000 per violation. The litigation remains unresolved, with a preliminary-injunction hearing scheduled for August 19.
