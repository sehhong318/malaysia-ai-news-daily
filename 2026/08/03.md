#### 2. Malaysia considers conditional easing of raw rare-earth export restrictions

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia, carrying Bloomberg reporting](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/813030)
- **Published:** 2026-08-03
- **Why it matters:** Any relaxation could alter Malaysia's strategy for capturing more value from critical minerals while affecting global efforts to diversify rare-earth supply beyond China.

Deputy Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability Minister Syed Ibrahim Syed Noh said authorities are assessing whether to allow some exports of unprocessed rare earths after imposing a moratorium in 2024 to encourage domestic processing. He said any exports would carry conditions tied to inbound investment, technology transfer and overseas research and development, but gave no timeline for a regulatory change. Malaysia has not announced that the restrictions have been lifted.

### AI News

#### 1. INTERPOL says AI enables 55% of reported cybercrime across Africa

- **Source:** [INTERPOL](https://www.interpol.int/News-and-Events/News/2026/INTERPOL-report-finds-AI-linked-to-more-than-half-of-cybercrime-in-Africa)
- **Published:** 2026-08-03
- **Why it matters:** The assessment links AI to cybercrime at regional scale and highlights an urgent need for cross-border enforcement, stronger data sharing and law-enforcement AI literacy.

INTERPOL's 2026 African Cyberthreat Assessment says artificial intelligence enables 55% of reported cybercrimes across Africa by making attacks faster, more scalable and harder to detect. Based on survey data from 36 member countries, the report says cybercrime-related losses more than doubled from US$192 million to US$484 million since 2024, while 72% of surveyed countries reported scam centres. These are reported losses and survey findings rather than a complete measure of all cybercrime in Africa.

#### 2. Sleep-study foundation model identifies patient groups with different long-term health risks

- **Source:** [Nature Communications paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75326-9); [Cleveland Clinic](https://newsroom.clevelandclinic.org/2026/08/03/ai-identifies-previously-unrecognized-health-insights-in-routine-sleep-studies)
- **Published:** 2026-08-03
- **Why it matters:** The work suggests AI could extract clinically useful risk signals from routine physiological tests that conventional summary measures leave unused, though prospective validation is still needed before clinical deployment.

Researchers developed a foundation model using polysomnography data and grouped patients into five risk categories, with the highest-risk group showing twice the five-year mortality risk of the lowest-risk group. Cleveland Clinic said the distinction was not captured by the apnea-hypopnea index and that the model's outcome predictions were independently confirmed in a nationwide patient cohort. The team says its next step is validation in diverse populations, so the findings should not yet be treated as a clinical decision tool.
