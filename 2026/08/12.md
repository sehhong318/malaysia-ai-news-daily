#### 2. Google's AMIE conducts real-time audio-visual clinical consultations in a simulated study

- **Published:** 2026-08-11

#### 3. Stanford study finds only 9% of registered California data brokers fully met transparency rules

- **Source:** [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/companies-that-buy-and-sell-your-data-are-not-following-californias-strict-privacy-laws)
- **Published:** 2026-08-11
- **Why it matters:** Weak enforcement of data-broker rules leaves personal information readily available for AI profiling, targeting and inference despite formal privacy rights.

Stanford RegLab and HAI researchers manually reviewed privacy policies for all 522 data brokers self-registered in California in 2025. They found 9% fully reported required transparency metrics, 45% submitted no rights-request metrics and 64% added friction such as confusing forms or excessive verification. The findings concern compliance with California's Delete Act; they do not measure every broker that may operate without registering.

#### 5. USF machine-learning system identifies movement patterns preceding ACL ruptures

- **Source:** [University of South Florida](https://www.usf.edu/news/2026/usf-researchers-develop-ai-system-that-predicts-acl-injuries-before-they-happen.aspx)
- **Published:** 2026-08-11
- **Why it matters:** Real-time warning of high-risk biomechanics could help athletes correct movements before injury, if the laboratory results generalise to varied sports and bodies.

University of South Florida researchers combined biomechanics and machine learning to identify features associated with anterior cruciate ligament rupture. USF reports 86% accuracy distinguishing pre-rupture from rupture conditions and 90% accuracy for conditions immediately preceding rupture, with a future wearable sleeve envisioned to provide haptic warnings. Funding, expanded testing and commercial development are still needed; the system predicts risky movement patterns rather than injuries with certainty.
