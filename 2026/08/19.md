#### 6. MIT study finds “attribution decay” in large image-generation datasets

- **Source:** [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/when-ai-art-has-no-author-generated-images-often-cant-be-traced-to-training-data-0818)
- **Published:** 2026-08-18
- **Why it matters:** If individual training examples cease to measurably affect outputs at scale, technical attribution may not provide the simple creator-credit mechanism that courts and licensing systems seek.

MIT CSAIL researchers built a “diffusion ensemble” that can exactly remove training examples without full retraining and compared 24 ensembles with conventional diffusion models. Across datasets ranging from 256 to more than 160,000 images, they found that removing a single image—or in some tests every image by one artist or of one person—often did not appreciably change the generated output, a phenomenon they call attribution decay. The result addresses causal influence on particular outputs; it does not determine whether collecting or training on the source material was lawful or ethical.
