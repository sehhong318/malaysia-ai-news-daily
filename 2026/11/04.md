### Malaysia News

#### 1. Malaysia declines India's request to extradite Zakir Naik

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/824320)
- **Published:** 2026-11-03
- **Why it matters:** The parliamentary disclosure settles the government's present position on a long-running bilateral legal issue, while leaving Malaysia responsible for explaining how treaty grounds were applied.

The Home Ministry told the Dewan Rakyat that Malaysia has decided not to extradite preacher Zakir Naik and made no commitment to do so, citing its rights under Article 6 of the 2010 Malaysia-India extradition treaty and the Extradition Act 1992. It said India submitted an official request on January 19, 2018 and a diplomatic note seeking reconsideration on May 29, 2020, but Malaysia has received no new request; the decision is Malaysia's stated legal position and does not adjudicate the cases Indian authorities allege against Naik.

#### 2. MCMC reports removing 60,632 cyberbullying-related posts since 2022

- **Source:** [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/11/04/mcmc-removed-over-60000-cyberbullying-posts-since-2022-parliament-told)
- **Published:** 2026-11-04
- **Why it matters:** The figures quantify the scale of Malaysia's online-content enforcement, making transparency, legal safeguards and freedom-of-expression oversight increasingly consequential.

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said in a written parliamentary reply that service providers removed 60,632 bullying-related items at MCMC's request between 2022 and September 15, 2026. He also reported 1,277 defamatory items and 55,895 false-content items removed, while the Safe Internet Campaign reached more than 2.8 million participants by September 30; these are ministry-reported enforcement and outreach totals, not an independent assessment of accuracy, proportionality or impact.

#### 3. Malaysia's US$1.5 billion Global Sukuk drew more than US$9.5 billion in demand

- **Source:** [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/11/04/malaysias-us15b-global-sukuk-over-subscribed-47-times-parliament-told)
- **Published:** 2026-11-04
- **Why it matters:** Strong demand lowered the government's borrowing spread, but the issuance still adds debt that must be managed within statutory and fiscal limits.

Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim said in a written parliamentary reply that the July 2026 two-tranche Global Sukuk was 4.7 times oversubscribed, enabling the government to tighten both tranches' spreads by 30 basis points from initial guidance. The 5.75-year tranche yielded 4.612% and the 10-year tranche 4.949%; after the issuance, offshore federal debt stood at RM23 billion, or about 1.7% of total federal debt and below the RM35 billion Foreign Loans Act limit.

#### 4. Putrajaya tightens grid-access conditions to curb data-centre “phantom demand”

- **Source:** [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/11/04/as-data-centre-power-share-heads-for-21pc-putrajaya-moves-to-avoid-phantom-demand-hogging-malaysias-grid/241892)
- **Published:** 2026-11-04
- **Why it matters:** Phased, binding grid commitments can prevent speculative projects from locking up scarce capacity as data-centre electricity demand rapidly expands.

The Energy Transition and Water Transformation Ministry said in a parliamentary reply that Peninsular Malaysia data-centre applicants must submit 10-year staggered demand plans, pass technical evaluations and sign binding electricity-supply agreements with TNB. Contracted capacity was 2,346 MW as of August but active sites used 1,374 MW, while data-centre consumption is forecast to rise from 7% of peninsular demand in 2026 to 21% in 2030; the ministry did not detail penalties or a formal process for reclaiming unused allocations.

Some ELITE lanes reopened after water receded, but access from the Guthrie Corridor Expressway towards Shah Alam remained blocked; conditions were evolving, so motorists were advised to defer non-essential travel and check live routing.

#### 1. Google releases a physical-engineering benchmark where every tested model scores below 50%

- **Source:** [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-physical-engineerings-last-exam-for-agents)
- **Published:** 2026-11-04
- **Why it matters:** Long-horizon tasks with code, safety and cost constraints test whether agents can produce reviewable engineering work rather than merely answer textbook questions.

Google and Mercor introduced Physical-Engineering's Last Exam, comprising 85 mechanical, electrical, civil and structural engineering tasks built around project files, standards and production deliverables. Google released 29 tasks and grading tools and reported that the best of 14 tested frontier models scored 48.3% on the private set, while only 41.3% of the leading configuration's deliverables passed every hard safety and feasibility check; these are organiser-reported baseline results that still require independent scrutiny.

#### 6. AI data centres turn to converted jet engines as grid waits lengthen

- **Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/data-centers-are-turning-to-old-jet-engines-as-the-wait-for-power-grows/)
- **Published:** 2026-11-03
- **Why it matters:** Emergency on-site generation shows that electricity access is becoming a binding AI-compute constraint, with efficiency and emissions costs shifted outside ordinary grid planning.

It cited eight converted units supporting xAI's Colossus 2 site and 13 ordered for OpenAI's Abilene Stargate campus; the approach can accelerate energisation but is less efficient and more polluting than conventional gas turbines, making it a costly workaround rather than evidence of a durable power solution.
