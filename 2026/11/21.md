#### 2. APEC Leaders Adopt Shenzhen Outlook on Trade, AI and Energy

- **Source:** [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3375120/apec-leaders-agree-joint-statement-pledges-boost-trade-supply-chain-resilience?module=latest&pgtype=homepage)
- **Published:** 2026-11-19
- **Why it matters:** The joint document sets the region's agenda on supply chains, energy security and emerging tech — the framework Malaysia's open-trade push will now operate in.

Leaders of the 21 APEC member economies adopted a joint document titled the Shenzhen Outlook late on Thursday, 19 November, after their two-day meeting in Shenzhen, breaking with the customary practice of issuing a leaders' declaration. The document reaffirms commitments to supply chain resilience, particularly for essential goods, and to energy security, including uninterrupted, reliable, affordable and sustainable access to energy. On trade, leaders pledged to keep facilitating cross-border flows through paperless trade and cross-border e-commerce, and to deepen cooperation on the digital economy and emerging technologies, including safeguards on data protection, online scams and the malicious use of AI. The document makes no mention of the World Trade Organization or the multilateral trading system.

#### 4. Malaysia Draws RM9.2 Billion in Indicative Investment Proposals from China

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/826545)
- **Published:** 2026-11-19
- **Why it matters:** The APEC trip produced a concrete investment pipeline in semiconductors, batteries and advanced materials — though the figure is indicative and delivery is the real test.

Malaysia secured indicative investment proposals worth RM9.2 billion following meetings with more than seven China-based companies during Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's visit to Shenzhen for the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, he said on Thursday, 19 November. - **Published:** 2026-11-19
- **Why it matters:** Malaysia's 5G story moves from building the network to extracting value from it, with enterprise adoption and AI applications as the next test.

Digital Nasional Bhd (DNB) is shifting its focus towards maximising returns from its nationwide 5G network, using a RM5.2 billion Islamic financing obtained recently. The funding will be used to strengthen network resilience, expand capacity and accelerate commercial adoption across businesses and the public sector. #### 3. Moonshot AI Cancels US$150 Million Nvidia Chip Deal After US Closes Remote-Access Loophole

- **Source:** [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/world/2026/11/21/moonshot-ai-scraps-nvidia-chip-deal-despite-50-percent)
- **Published:** 2026-11-21
- **Why it matters:** The first visible casualty of the new US chip rules hits Southeast Asian data centres directly — the same rental route Malaysia's data-centre sector has been warned about.

Chinese AI company Moonshot AI has cancelled a US$150 million contract to lease Nvidia Blackwell Ultra B300 chips through a cloud provider in Indonesia, The Information reported on 19 November, citing sources. Moonshot had planned to access the chips — which the US bars from direct sale to China — from next year, and had reportedly agreed to pay 50% of the contract value upfront, more than triple the customary 15%. The cancellation follows a US Commerce Department rule announced on 16 November and effective on 19 November that requires licences for data centres providing significant computing power to Chinese customers, and adds licence requirements for advanced chip shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. Moonshot has also reportedly halted talks on new contracts; the company has not publicly confirmed the cancellation.

- **Source:** [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/essentially-unusable-openai-launches-gpt-6-2-astra-and-sol-to-negative-reception-from-ai-power-users)
- **Published:** 2026-11-18
- **Why it matters:** Benchmark gains are not landing with the power users OpenAI needs most, raising questions about its agent-first direction a week after well-received rival launches.

OpenAI released its new models GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on Tuesday, 17 November, with Astra priced at US$8 per million input tokens and US$40 per million output tokens — 20% cheaper than its predecessor — while Sol kept the previous generation's US$2 and US$10 rates. Several prominent early testers responded sharply: investor Matt Shumer called the models "essentially unusable", developer Theo Browne called Astra "the worst model OpenAI has ever released", and Wharton professor Ethan Mollick found it "very uneven & weird" and harder to steer than rivals. The criticism centred on delegated work and long projects — models stopping early, looping without progress, and producing hard-to-follow explanations — though testers also praised Astra's 3D work, code review and computer-use abilities, and the reaction was not unanimous. OpenAI's own launch charts show Astra beating its predecessor on all nine benchmarks, but independent Codex testing found a flat result for Astra and a regression for Sol.

#### 6. Google's Gemini Can Now Operate Mac Apps in the Background

- **Source:** [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2322728/google-boosts-geminis-ability-to-do-repetitive-tasks-on-its-mac-app/)
- **Published:** 2026-11-20
- **Why it matters:** The desktop-agent race reaches the Mac: Google is betting that AI which does multi-step work across apps matters more than chatbots that only answer.

Google has added its Computer Use capability to the Gemini app for Mac, letting the AI see what is on screen via screenshots and click, type, scroll and navigate programs and websites on the user's behalf. The work runs in an isolated background window with a live preview in the chat, so the user's own screen and cursor stay free, and Gemini only uses apps it has been given access to, asking for approval for key actions. Google's examples include batch-listing items for sale on eBay from local photos, filling out shipping forms from PDFs, and turning a pencil sketch into a 3D model. The feature is rolling out in English in the US only, for users 18 and over with personal Google accounts on Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra plans, on Apple Silicon Macs running macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later.
