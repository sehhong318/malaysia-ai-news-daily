- **Why it matters:** Allowing dual-career households to remain economically active could improve Malaysia's ability to attract specialised workers, although the narrow eligibility and pending conditions may limit its reach.

#### 6. Malaysia's manufacturing PMI returns to growth territory

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/824108)
- **Published:** 2026-11-02
- **Why it matters:** Renewed output and order growth points to firmer fourth-quarter industrial activity, while rising costs and weaker confidence show that the recovery remains exposed to supply and geopolitical pressures.

The seasonally adjusted S&P Global Malaysia Manufacturing PMI rose from 49.9 in September to 51.0 in October, its strongest improvement since April. Output grew for the first time in three months, new orders increased at one of the fastest rates in four and a half years, and employment expanded for a third month. Business confidence nevertheless fell to a six-month low, input delays persisted and transport and material costs increased; PMI is a survey indicator rather than an official measure of realised GDP.

#### 4. KAIST method cuts tested fine-tuning time by up to 69.6%

- **Source:** [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/122357/kaist-develops-technology-to-cut-ai-fine-tuning-time-by-up-to-69-6-percent)
- **Published:** 2026-11-02
- **Why it matters:** Reducing redundant examples could lower the compute cost of adapting language models for medicine and coding without requiring additional GPUs.

KAIST researchers introduced CluSTER, which groups examples by their effect on training, selects diverse samples and distributes them across GPUs. Tests on roughly 7-billion- and 13-billion-parameter models, three coding and medical datasets and eight evaluations used about 31% of the full data and reduced training time by as much as 69.6% while largely preserving performance. The results were presented at EMNLP and tested mainly on four- and eight-GPU setups, so they do not prove the same savings for every model, dataset or production workload.

#### 5. StreamVibe benchmark tests when a video assistant should intervene

- **Source:** [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-wants-video-ai-to-understand-what-people-need-as-they-workgoogle-researchers-are-teaching-a-video-assistant-when-to-offer-help/)
- **Published:** 2026-11-01
- **Why it matters:** Proactive assistants need to infer when help is useful without becoming distracting, a capability that ordinary question-answer benchmarks do not measure.

A Google-led research team built StreamVibe around roughly 4,100 approved examples drawn from more than 183 hours of everyday and software video, including explicit, vague and changing requests. The team trained StreamVibe-VL to decide each second whether to speak and used a separate memory system to track pending requests and completed steps; it reported a 10.7% improvement in response timing and quality over comparison models. The article also reports that all tested models lost conversational context during continuous use, underscoring that the benchmark result is not evidence of reliable deployment.
