#### 1. MCMC and MACC agree to use AI in enforcement and corruption prevention

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/820379)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** Cross-agency use of AI could improve detection of digital misconduct, but accountability will depend on concrete safeguards, validation and oversight that the memorandum itself does not yet specify.

The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission and Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission signed a memorandum covering coordinated enforcement, investigation, digital technology, information management and corruption prevention. MCMC said the agencies intend to use AI to detect and manage risks of abuse of power and misappropriation, while also expanding training and knowledge-sharing. The agreement establishes a cooperation framework; the cited statement does not identify deployed systems, performance evidence or rules governing automated analysis.

#### 2. High Court temporarily stops Pahang enforcement against Berjaya Hills land

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/820318)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** The dispute tests state authority over quit-rent rates and land forfeiture while preserving access to affected Bukit Tinggi facilities until the courts consider the company's challenges.

The Temerloh High Court granted Berjaya Hills Resorts an interim stay against enforcement over 122 land parcels and ordered buildings unsealed and utilities restored pending a substantive stay hearing on November 27. The company is challenging forfeiture proceedings linked to RM70 million in quit rent and has sought Federal Court permission to appeal a July ruling that states may set quit-rent rates without National Land Council approval; that leave hearing is scheduled for December 8. The temporary orders do not decide whether the rates or forfeitures are lawful.

#### 3. Penang contractor denies allegation that it moved AI chips to China

- **Source:** [Free Malaysia Today via MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-my/technology/tech-companies/penang-based-company-denies-moving-ai-chips-to-china/ar-AA2ds3n1)
- **Published:** 2026-10-03
- **Why it matters:** Allegations that Malaysia is used to reroute export-controlled AI hardware could affect trade compliance, enforcement scrutiny and the country's electronics supply-chain reputation.

Penang-based Innospec denied a *New York Times* allegation that it moved AI chips to China, calling the claim untrue. The company said it assembles a product only when the customer consigns the required materials. This is the company's response to a published allegation, not a court or regulator finding, and the cited report does not independently resolve the underlying shipment history.

#### 4. Survey finds US manufacturers in Malaysia shifting inputs away from China

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/820295)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** Tariff-driven localisation may create orders for Malaysian suppliers while showing how trade restrictions are reorganising regional manufacturing networks.

An American Malaysian Chamber of Commerce survey found that nine of 21 manufacturers sourcing from China reduced that reliance after US tariffs, while none increased it; 15 of 44 manufacturing respondents increased the Malaysian share of their inputs. The survey covered 84 member companies, with responses collected from March to May, and reported RM19.32 billion in payments to 7,087 local suppliers during 2024–2025. These are member-survey results rather than comprehensive statistics for all US-linked firms in Malaysia.

#### 5. Business groups ask government to defer another minimum-wage increase

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/820384)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** The request sharpens the policy trade-off between worker incomes and smaller firms' claims about affordability before Budget 2027.

The SME Association of Malaysia and Malaysia Retail Chain Association asked the government to defer any increase from the current RM1,700 minimum wage and phase in a future rise. The SME association said 95.8% of 333 respondents to its quick survey preferred a delay, while the government has said other measures—including Progressive Wage Policy subsidies—will support wage growth and that further measures will be announced in the October 9 budget. The survey represents participating businesses' views; it is not a government decision or a worker-representative assessment.

#### 6. Batu flood-retention pond study considers 10 million litres of daily water supply

- **Source:** [Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/general/news.php?id=2614534)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** A dual-purpose pond could add drought resilience and local supply without new storage, provided water abstraction does not weaken flood protection or compromise water quality.

The Energy Transition and Water Transformation Ministry said a pilot under study could supply five million litres per day for domestic use—potentially serving about 6,000 homes—and another five million for industry. The National Water Research Institute is due to complete a feasibility study and concept design in December, examining flood capacity, pollution and treatability before any physical works proceed subject to government approval. The 10-million-litre figure is a proposal, not operating capacity already available.

### AI News

#### 1. Canada creates a 13-member national AI advisory council

- **Source:** [Office of the Prime Minister of Canada](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/10/02/prime-minister-carney-launches-new-national-council-artificial)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** A standing council can connect safety, adoption and sovereign-infrastructure policy, although its influence will depend on transparency and whether government acts on its advice.

Prime Minister Mark Carney launched a National Council on Artificial Intelligence to advise the Canadian government on deploying AI, managing risks and updating the national strategy. Its 13 members include Yoshua Bengio, Sanja Fidler, Gillian Hadfield and leaders from Canadian investment, cloud, research and public institutions. The body is advisory rather than a new regulator and does not itself create binding rules.

#### 2. Anthropic CEO asks governments for power to block unsafe model deployment

- **Source:** [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Business/exclusive-anthropic-ceo-calls-stronger-regulation-ai/story?id=133753620)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** A frontier-lab chief explicitly backing narrow government veto authority raises the policy baseline beyond voluntary company commitments.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei told ABC News that governments should be able to block deployment when third-party assessment finds unacceptable risk, while saying primary responsibility still rests with developers. He also announced US$200 million for research into AI's societal effects and discussed labour-transition measures, including wage insurance and stronger unemployment support. These are Anthropic's policy proposals and risk judgments, not adopted legislation or proof that international agreement on a pause is feasible.

#### 3. Apple says macOS will tighten Full Disk Access as agent risks grow

- **Source:** [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=p6zjojqw)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** Desktop agents can reach messages, mail, browsing history and files, making operating-system permission design a central privacy safeguard rather than an app-only concern.

Apple announced additional controls so granting Full Disk Access will require more explicit user action, warning that some developers use the permission in ways users may not fully understand. The permission can expose files, mail, messages and browsing history, and Apple said increasingly autonomous agents amplify the risk. The announcement sets a platform direction but does not yet establish how effectively the revised interface will prevent mistaken or coerced consent.

#### 4. Meta opens Muse to do-it-yourself hardware projects

- **Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1004330/meta-muse-ai-gadgets-home-link)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** Extending a personal agent to sensors, displays and home controls broadens experimentation while increasing the consequences of insecure permissions or unreliable actions.

Meta released open-source firmware and software development kits that let developers connect Muse to ESP32 boards, Raspberry Pi systems, displays, buttons, sensors and actuators. It also produced 5,000 Muse Home Link devices that use community-built skills to interact with equipment such as lights, televisions and printers, with shipping planned during October. Meta explicitly warns builders to proceed at their own risk, and the release does not demonstrate that third-party projects are secure or dependable.

#### 5. Amazon reportedly explores an US$8 billion AI-chip sale-and-leaseback

- **Source:** [SiliconANGLE, reporting Financial Times findings](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/02/report-amazon-to-offload-8b-worth-of-ai-chips-lease-them-back/)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** Moving costly accelerators and debt into a special-purpose vehicle would show how hyperscalers are using financial engineering to sustain enormous AI infrastructure spending.

The *Financial Times*, cited by SiliconANGLE, reported that Amazon is discussing transferring about US$8 billion of Nvidia Grace Blackwell accelerators to a special-purpose vehicle that would issue debt and sell a 10% equity stake to outside investors. Amazon would continue using the chips under a lease, removing both assets and associated financing from its balance sheet. The discussions are reported plans rather than a completed transaction, and neither final terms nor investor commitments were confirmed in the cited report.

#### 6. Nvidia introduces a 64GB DGX Spark for local AI workloads

- **Source:** [NVIDIA](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/local-ai-dgx-spark-64gb-sync/)
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Why it matters:** A lower-memory desktop system and simple two-node clustering could widen private local model use, although capacity and performance remain below larger workstations and cloud clusters.

Nvidia announced a 64GB unified-memory DGX Spark configuration from six hardware partners, due October 23 from US$4,999. The company says one system supports models up to 100 billion parameters, while two units can pool 128GB and support up to 200 billion parameters through its Sync Cluster Assistant; it reported up to 1.7-times performance in a selected two-node Qwen test. Model limits and speed comparisons are vendor claims and will vary with quantisation, context, workload and software configuration.
