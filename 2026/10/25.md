#### 7. Singapore widens its transboundary-haze inquiry to 10 locally registered companies

- **Source:** [The Malaysian Reserve/Bloomberg](https://themalaysianreserve.com/2026/10/24/singapore-expands-haze-investigation-to-10-locally-based-firms/)
- **Published:** 2026-10-24
- **Why it matters:** A supply-chain investigation and possible enforcement in Singapore could add regional accountability pressure over fires contributing to unhealthy air in Malaysia.

Singapore's National Environment Agency requested concession, fire-prevention and corporate-relationship information from 10 Singapore-registered palm-oil or forestry companies, after earlier requests to 10 Indonesian companies. The inquiry will examine whether companies caused or condoned fires linked to the regional haze; Singapore's Transboundary Haze Act permits fines of up to S$100,000 per day. Notices and an investigation are not findings of responsibility, and Singapore has not yet imposed a penalty under the law.

#### 2. Meta delays new business terms after publishers object to possible AI scraping

- **Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017450/meta-business-terms-delayed-ai-scraping)
- **Published:** 2026-10-23
- **Why it matters:** The dispute tests whether platform contracts and robots.txt provide publishers meaningful control over the use of their work in AI systems.

Meta postponed enforcement of revised business terms from October 30 to November 20 after publisher groups argued that the language could permit scraping news sites for AI training. Meta said the delay would allow it to explain its web crawlers and content owners' ability to signal preferences through robots.txt; it had already removed the word “training,” but legal experts cited by The Verge said that did not rule out AI-related use. The postponement does not settle how the terms will ultimately be interpreted or enforced.
