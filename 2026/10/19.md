#### 1. Nature perspective argues for limiting AI growth on sustainability grounds

- **Source:** [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02617-y)
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Why it matters:** The article puts compute, ownership and energy use inside the wider debate over whether AI development can be reconciled with climate and resource limits.

Authors Matthias Schmelzer and Steffen Lange argue that the current AI growth model depends on enclosed data commons, fossil energy and competitive pressure that makes precaution difficult. #### 2. EASE-Fact models event order and source reliability in short-video news screening

- **Source:** [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-76343-w)
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Why it matters:** Screening video claims as evidence arrives could help editors prioritise review without treating a model score as a final determination of truth.

The paper introduces EASE-Fact, which tracks ordered visual events and uses a reliability-aware router across titles, images, speech and captions; a streaming version limits itself to evidence available at each point in time. The authors evaluated it chronologically and across disjoint events and added historical debunking records only when temporally available. They explicitly distinguish benchmark predictions and detected internal conflicts from independent fact-checking, so real-world reliability remains to be established.

#### 3. Compact speech-restoration models address bandwidth, noise and reverberation separately

- **Source:** [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73824-w)
- **Published:** 2026-10-18
- **Why it matters:** A 4.8-million-parameter design could make speech enhancement more practical on constrained hardware, but controlled tests do not establish field performance.

Researchers used a common encoder–BLSTM–decoder architecture in three separately trained models for bandwidth limitation, additive noise and reverberation. On generated degraded–clean speech pairs, the 4.8-million-parameter framework reported PESQ scores of 2.95–3.05 and STOI of about 0.86 while using fewer parameters than the compared systems. The results come from controlled task-specific evaluations, not diverse live acoustic environments or a single universal restoration model.
