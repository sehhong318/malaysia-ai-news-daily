#### 2. Anthropic demonstrates a distributed self-replicating AI worm on smart televisions

- **Source:** [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/18/anthropic-ai-worm-worries)
- **Published:** 2026-10-18
- **Why it matters:** Distributed autonomy across poorly secured consumer devices could turn familiar malware propagation into a more adaptive threat, although the demonstrated system is not yet capable of operating as a practical wild worm.

Anthropic's offensive-research team built a program that spread autonomously across 15 internet-connected televisions and split its workload among them. The researchers said a successful real-world AI worm still needs more capability on low-power devices than is currently available, but projected that trend lines could make it feasible by year-end; that timing is the team's forecast, not an observed outbreak.

#### 3. Google releases Harness² code for agents that refine their own scaffolding

- **Source:** [Google Research on GitHub](https://github.com/google-research/harness2)
- **Published:** 2026-10-14
- **Why it matters:** Open code for changing prompts, tools, memory and orchestration makes agent self-improvement reproducible without altering model weights, while benchmark gains do not establish safe or reliable deployment.

Google Research published Harness² under Apache 2.0, implementing recursive changes to eight surrounding agent components through a Propose–Probe–Compose process while keeping model weights fixed. Its README reports single-step gains of up to 11.5 points on WorkBuddy-Bench, 4.5 on JobBench and 11.8 percentage points on LAB's partial pass rate across tested configurations; the figures are developer-reported benchmark results.

#### 4. Ugandan university survey links frequent AI use to grades, not measured competencies

- **Source:** [Bioengineer.org](https://bioengineer.org/ai-use-linked-to-better-grades-but-not-deeper-skills-in-ugandan-universities/)
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Why it matters:** The divergence warns universities that higher marks associated with AI use may not translate into the deeper skills competence-based education is intended to build.

A cross-sectional survey of 565 staff and students at five Ugandan universities found a moderate correlation between AI-use frequency and academic performance (r=0.544) but an almost negligible correlation with competency attainment (r=0.045). Because recruitment used convenience sampling and the design was observational and self-reported, the results show associations rather than proving that AI caused either outcome.

#### 6. Survey finds a large gap between Anthropic staff and US public expectations for AI

- **Source:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ai-expectations-gap)
- **Published:** 2026-10-16
- **Why it matters:** The disagreement helps explain radically different economic forecasts, while the unusually exposed employee sample may be systematically more bullish than the wider public.

Staff expected AI could perform 75% of their own work hours by 2030 versus 28% among US workers, and also anticipated wider adoption and larger time savings; these are beliefs about future capability and use, not measured forecasts or consensus estimates.
