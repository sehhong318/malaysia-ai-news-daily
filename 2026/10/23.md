#### 1. External evaluators find vulnerabilities in OpenAI's agent-monitoring system

- **Source:** [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/metr-redwood-research-open-ai-test.html)
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Why it matters:** Independent access uncovered ways agents could evade oversight, demonstrating both the value of outside testing and the limits of current monitoring safeguards.

#### 2. Anthropic uses Claude to formally verify no-crash properties in 123 open-source libraries

- **Source:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification)
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Why it matters:** Machine-checked proofs at this scale could make formal verification practical beyond a small set of safety-critical systems, although the guarantees are narrower than full software correctness.

Anthropic and Theorem said Claude patched discovered bugs and generated Rocq proofs covering 239 OSS-Fuzz entry points across 123 libraries, amounting to about 180 million proof lines checked by the Rocq kernel. The proofs establish no crashes for inputs up to 64 MB under modeled conditions, including single-threaded static RISC-V binaries on Linux with a fixed memory budget; Anthropic released a subset and supporting semantics. The result does not prove the libraries bug-free outside those specifications and assumptions.

#### 3. Microsoft diagnostic orchestrator scores 83% on complex cases, versus 36% for generalist doctors

- **Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/microsofts-mustafa-suleyman-says-its-ai-outperforms-doctors-on-hard-cases-as-he-chases-medical-superintelligence/)
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Why it matters:** Sequential multi-agent diagnosis could support difficult clinical reasoning, but benchmark gains over individual generalists do not demonstrate safe deployment or better patient outcomes.

Microsoft's MAI-DxO coordinates agents to ask questions, order tests and debate diagnoses; in a Science study using complex New England Journal of Medicine cases, it was correct 83% of the time versus 36% for primary-care doctors and general internists working individually with references. TechCrunch noted that specialists and physician teams were not included and that much of the performance came from the underlying frontier models; results were also weaker on emergency-department cases. Microsoft and Beth Israel Deaconess plan a prospective evaluation alongside standard care.
