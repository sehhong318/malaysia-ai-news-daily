#### 2. Malaysia restarts an IAEA-aligned early assessment of nuclear power

- **Source:** [New Straits Times](https://www.nst.com.my/business/economy/2026/09/1530210/malaysias-revived-nuclear-ambition-could-see-first-plant-2040s)
- **Published:** 2026-09-11
- **Why it matters:** Nuclear power is returning to Malaysia's long-term energy-security debate as data-centre electricity demand rises, but deployment would require years of regulatory, financing and workforce preparation.

Malaysia has restarted Phase 1 of an early-stage assessment under the International Atomic Energy Agency framework, coordinated by the Malaysia Programme Office for Power Electricity Reform. CIMB Securities analyst Muhammad Afif Zulkaplly estimated that a decision could come in 2030 or 2031 and a first plant might operate between 2040 and 2046; large reactors, small modular reactors and floating plants remain among the options. These dates are an analyst's scenario, and the government has not decided to adopt nuclear energy or selected a technology.

#### 5. NASA and IBM open-source a lunar-science foundation model

- **Published:** 2026-09-10
- **Why it matters:** Open weights, code, data and benchmarks can accelerate reproducible AI research on lunar geology and resource mapping ahead of future missions.

NASA and IBM released a model trained on roughly two million lunar image tiles, including more than one million one-metre-resolution camera images and nearly 964,000 multispectral images at 100-metre resolution. It can be adapted with limited labelled data for crater mapping, volcanic-feature detection and estimating polar-ice stability; NASA said it matched or exceeded selected baselines across evaluated tasks and performed best on ice-stability estimation. The model, codebase, machine-learning-ready data and benchmarks are public, but the reported evaluation still needs broader independent replication.
