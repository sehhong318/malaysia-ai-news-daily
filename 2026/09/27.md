#### 8. Experiment finds AI advice sharply reduces willingness to answer “I don't know”

- **Source:** [The Decoder, reporting a five-experiment study](https://the-decoder.com/ai-access-makes-people-almost-entirely-unwilling-to-say-i-dont-know-study-finds/)
- **Published:** 2026-09-26
- **Why it matters:** AI interfaces may amplify confident error by changing human judgement even when users can abstain and the model is systematically unreliable.

Across five experiments with 3,132 participants, access to a language model reduced abstention from 36%–44% in no-AI groups to 3%–6% in the first two studies; in one condition, accuracy fell from 27.6% to 10% while reported confidence rose. Researchers deliberately selected obscure movie-detail questions on which the model was usually wrong, and modest financial incentives did not eliminate the effect. The narrow trivia setting limits generalisation to professional decisions, and the findings require replication across tasks and models.
