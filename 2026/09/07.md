#### 3. Moody's sees energy-export buffer but tighter liquidity at Malaysian Islamic banks

- **Source:** [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/09/07/islamic-lenders-buffered-by-malaysias-net-energy-exporter-status)
- **Published:** 2026-09-07
- **Why it matters:** The assessment suggests the banking system can absorb the energy shock, but high funding needs could make Islamic lenders more sensitive to household and SME stress.

Moody's Ratings said Malaysia's net liquefied-natural-gas exporter status, diversified oil sourcing and domestic demand should make the Middle East conflict manageable for its Islamic banks. It nevertheless identified tighter liquidity than at conventional peers: rapid financing growth had lifted the sector's financing-to-deposit ratio to 113% at the end of 2025, while higher living and business costs could weigh on retail and SME borrowers. Moody's expects capital buffers to remain adequate and liquidity pressure to ease as financing growth moderates.
