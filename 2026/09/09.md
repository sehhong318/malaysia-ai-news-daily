#### 2. Malaysia's first-seven-month trade rises 24.7% to RM2.16 trillion

- **Source:** [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/09/08/malaysias-trade-rises-247-to-rm216-trillion-in-first-seven-months-of-2026)
- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** Rapid export growth and a larger surplus strengthen Malaysia's external position, with electronics and AI-related infrastructure demand providing much of the momentum.

MATRADE said exports increased 29.2% year on year to RM1.165 trillion in January–July, while the trade surplus more than doubled to RM170.50 billion and extended the run of monthly surpluses to 75 months. Electrical and electronics products contributed RM563.65 billion, or 48.4% of exports; shipments to the US rose 58.3%, China 24.3% and the EU 29.8%. The release reports nominal trade values and does not by itself separate price effects from real volume growth.

#### 3. Retail sales growth misses expectations at 2.5% in the second quarter

- **Source:** [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/817324)
- **Published:** 2026-09-09
- **Why it matters:** Cautious household spending despite cash aid and fuel subsidies points to continuing pressure on consumption and discretionary businesses.

The Malaysia Retail Industry Report put year-on-year retail-sales growth at 2.5% in the second quarter, below the 4.8% average forecast by members of two retail associations; first-half growth was 3.1%. The report said consumers made fewer shopping trips, compared prices and cut discretionary services, while retailers forecast 4.7% growth for the third quarter. That outlook is an industry projection rather than an assured recovery.

#### 4. PISA 2025 shows Malaysian gains in science and reading but a mathematics decline

- **Source:** [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/09/08/pisa-2025-malaysian-teens-score-better-in-science-reading-but-decline-in-maths)
- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** The mixed results identify mathematics as a policy weakness while showing unusually strong computational problem-solving performance.

Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek said Malaysia's science score rose three points from PISA 2022 to 419 and reading rose five points to 393, while mathematics fell 11 points to 397. Malaysia scored 484 in computational problem solving and was one of six systems reported to exceed OECD expectations in that domain; 5% of participating Malaysian students also reported cyberbullying. PISA measures sampled 15-year-olds and should not be read as a score for every student or school.

#### 5. Government projects 300,000–500,000 new AI jobs but warns of displacement risk

- **Source:** [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/09/09/ai-drive-expected-to-create-500000-new-jobs)
- **Published:** 2026-09-09
- **Why it matters:** Malaysia's AI strategy will be judged by whether infrastructure investment creates accessible skilled work faster than automation displaces existing roles.

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said the government expects its AI push to create 300,000 to 500,000 jobs and add 0.8–1.2 percentage points, or about RM13 billion–RM20 billion annually, to GDP growth by 2030. He contrasted those targets with a TalentCorp study estimating that 697,000 jobs could be heavily affected by AI, digitalisation and the green economy over three to five years without reskilling. The job and GDP figures are government targets, not realised outcomes.

#### 6. Data centres reach a record 9.3% of Peninsular electricity use during August heat

- **Source:** [TNGlobal](https://technode.global/2026/09/09/malaysia-data-centers-record-power-use-heat/)
- **Published:** 2026-09-09
- **Why it matters:** The measured peak shows that hot weather can amplify the grid impact of Malaysia's AI-compute expansion beyond annual-average demand assumptions.

Citing energy officials and Reuters, TNGlobal reported that data centres used a record 9.3% of Peninsular Malaysia's electricity in the second week of August, versus about 7% on average in 2026, as higher temperatures increased cooling loads. The Energy Commission estimates their share could reach as much as 31% by 2035, although officials said current demand remains within planning assumptions and does not indicate an immediate shortage. This adds a measured peak-demand update to the previous edition's report that Malaysia plans about 9GW of new gas-fired capacity by 2032.

- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** A large cloud customer gives Qualcomm a credible route into AI data centres and could broaden the accelerator market beyond Nvidia.

#### 2. Meta launches its Muse personal agent in the US

- **Source:** [Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)
- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** Giving a consumer agent access to communications, purchases and background tasks raises the practical consequences of failures far beyond ordinary chatbot errors.

Meta launched Muse through a dedicated app and WhatsApp, saying it can send emails, book travel, fill forms and make purchases while running in a dedicated virtual machine and asking for approval before selected actions. It is initially available in the US, with an encrypted “Confidential VM” version promised later in 2026. The privacy, security and reliability benefits are company claims that still require real-world validation.

- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** Treating distillation as potential trade-secret theft could lead to sanctions, export restrictions and a sharper split between US and Chinese model ecosystems.

These are US government allegations; CNN said it sought comment from the Chinese embassy and named companies, and the article did not report an adjudicated legal finding.

#### 4. OpenAI commits US$5 million to independent research on AI and teenagers

- **Source:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/teen-development-research-grants/)
- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** Independent evidence on adolescent development and safeguards is needed as generative AI becomes embedded in teenagers' learning, relationships and advice-seeking.

OpenAI opened applications for grants of up to US$1 million each, totalling up to US$5 million, for research on how generative AI affects people aged 13–17. OpenAI will fund and administer the programme, so disclosure and research independence will be important despite the stated preference for credible findings regardless of whether they favour providers.

#### 5. Hugging Face and Earthmover simplify running open AI weather models

- **Source:** [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/hugging-science/earthmover-hf)
- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** Easier access to models, initial conditions and validation data can broaden independent weather-model testing beyond organisations with large compute and storage budgets.

A joint tutorial and demo connect open models including Microsoft's Aurora with analysis-ready Earthmover weather data and Hugging Face Jobs. The authors report an end-to-end 24-hour forecast in under about 30 seconds in their demo and say Aurora can run on a CPU at two to three minutes per six-hour step without a GPU. These are workflow demonstrations rather than an independent comparison of forecast accuracy against operational physics-based systems.

#### 6. TIER IV releases an open end-to-end autonomous-driving model built with agentic AI

- **Source:** [TIER IV](https://tier4.co.jp/en/updates/technology/20260908-reference-e2e-model)
- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** Publishing code, weights and training recipes creates a testable baseline for whether agents can automate meaningful parts of safety-critical model development.

TIER IV released the code, trained model and recipe for METEOR, which uses surround-camera video to perform bird's-eye-view perception and trajectory generation without high-definition maps. The company says CoMET generated the labels and an AI agent ran the train–evaluate–improve loop, while Nvidia Cosmos supplied data augmentation for weather and rare cases. The “zero human labels, zero human code” description is vendor-reported, and the release does not establish safe autonomous operation on public roads.

#### 7. Sierra open-sources a benchmark for agents that build customer-service agents

- **Source:** [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/sierra-open-sources-hyper-tau-bench-a-benchmark-for-agent-construction/)
- **Published:** 2026-09-08
- **Why it matters:** Evaluating complete agent construction under hidden production tests is more demanding and operationally relevant than measuring isolated coding tasks.

Sierra released hyper-τ-bench under the MIT licence, with 53 tasks spanning airline, retail, telecom and banking domains and a public leaderboard. Its best automated configuration passed 23.9% of held-out tasks, compared with 82.2% for a reference engineer-plus-frontier-model pairing, according to Sierra. The scores are project-reported and should be independently reproduced before drawing broad conclusions about coding-agent capability.
