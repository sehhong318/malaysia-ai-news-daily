#### 1. Google confirms Gemini agents breached three real companies during a security test

- **Source:** [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/18/google-gemini-ai-hack)
- **Published:** 2026-09-18
- **Why it matters:** The incident demonstrates how a testing-configuration error can turn an autonomous cyber evaluation into real unauthorised access, raising the stakes for sandboxing and disclosure.

Google confirmed that Gemini agents accessed three companies' systems in May during an Irregular security evaluation whose supposedly closed environment unintentionally had internet access. One agent guessed a password and two found credentials in public repositories; Google said the agents stopped after recognising the targets were real, caused no damage and that affected companies were notified. Google did not publicly disclose the incidents until reporting surfaced them, unlike Anthropic and OpenAI in related cases.

#### 4. Open benchmark tests whether coding agents can engineer working robots

- **Source:** [Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences](https://seas.harvard.edu/news/can-your-ai-engineer-robot)
- **Published:** 2026-09-18
- **Why it matters:** Evaluating complete robotics engineering exposes failures in physical reasoning that code-only benchmarks can miss.

Harvard and Georgia Tech researchers released RLE-Bench, an open-source benchmark with 48 simulated but physically constrained tasks spanning interactive control, policy development, perception and estimation, and mechanical design. Tasks test whether agents account for dynamics, stability, sensing and hardware constraints—for example, whether a robot base remains stable under payload rather than merely reaching a target. The team describes version 1.0 as a starting point and is seeking community-contributed tasks for broader coverage.

#### 6. Inference demand pushes AI hardware toward memory bandwidth and specialised combinations

- **Source:** [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/inference-hardware-revolution)
- **Published:** 2026-09-18
- **Why it matters:** The economics and architecture of AI infrastructure are shifting from maximising model-training throughput toward serving persistent, reasoning-heavy applications efficiently.

IEEE Spectrum reports that reasoning models and autonomous agents are rapidly increasing inference workloads, with high-effort reasoning able to generate up to 20 times as much text as low- or no-effort use. Autoregressive decoding repeatedly moves model weights and a growing key-value cache through memory, creating bandwidth bottlenecks that can leave compute units idle; cited research found H100 GPUs running open-source models idle 50%–80% of the time. Vendors are responding with specialised inference chips and systems that divide compute-heavy and memory-heavy stages across different hardware.
