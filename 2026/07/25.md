### Malaysia News

#### 1. Malaysia targets first locally made human vaccine as early as 2028

- **Source:** [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/07/25/msia-made-vaccine-for-humans)
- **Published:** 2026-07-25
- **Why it matters:** Domestic vaccine manufacturing could reduce Malaysia's dependence on foreign suppliers during health emergencies and build strategic biotechnology capability.

Science, Technology and Innovation Minister Chang Lih Kang said Malaysia could produce its first locally made human vaccine as early as 2028, ahead of a 2030 capability target. Pharmaniaga is manufacturing a 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine through technology transfer from a Chinese company; the vaccine protects against 13 types of *Streptococcus pneumoniae* bacteria associated with illnesses including pneumonia and meningitis. Chang said Malaysia currently imports all of its vaccines and is also working on a hexavalent vaccine for children.

#### 2. US summons Malaysian ambassador over longstanding Israeli-entry policy

- **Source:** [Al Jazeera, reporting Anadolu, Reuters and Bernama](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/24/us-summons-malaysia-envoy-over-israel-policy)
- **Published:** 2026-07-24
- **Why it matters:** The formal diplomatic summons escalates a policy dispute that could put broader Malaysia-US relations under strain.

The US Department of State summoned Malaysia's ambassador, Muhammad Shahrul Ikram Yaakob, over Malaysia's policy of not recognising Israel. Foreign Minister Mohamad Hasan said the ambassador explained that the policy was longstanding and described a case in which a US-Israeli dual national entered on a US passport but was asked to leave after authorities identified the person's Israeli citizenship. This is a material update to the dispute covered on July 23: US lawmakers had sought a review of bilateral ties, and the State Department has now formally called in Malaysia's envoy.

#### 2. Google signs EU code for transparency of AI-generated content

- **Source:** [Google](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/public-policy/eu-ai-act-transparency-code-of-practice/)
- **Published:** 2026-07-24
- **Why it matters:** Google's participation could accelerate interoperable labelling and watermarking practices across widely used generative-AI services in Europe.

Google said it will sign the EU AI Act Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content, following its 2025 signing of the general-purpose AI code. The company linked the commitment to its adoption of the C2PA standard and SynthID watermarking, including interoperability work with Apple, ElevenLabs, Kakao, Nvidia and OpenAI. #### 4. Nvidia, Microsoft and other companies urge US lawmakers to protect open-weight AI

- **Source:** [Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/07/25/nvidia-microsoft-and-other-tech-giants-back-open-source-ai-models-)
- **Published:** 2026-07-25
- **Why it matters:** The intervention puts major technology vendors directly into a policy fight over whether open-weight models should face broad restrictions or targeted safeguards.

Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, IBM and about two dozen other companies and groups signed a letter asking US lawmakers to avoid what they called premature restrictions on open models. The signatories argued that concerns such as technology theft should be addressed through targeted legal and commercial measures, and that open weights allow outside researchers to identify vulnerabilities and develop safeguards. The letter arrives as US policymakers consider controls on Chinese model makers and proposed emergency shutdown authority following the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident.

#### 5. Researchers develop system to attribute fake videos to their AI generators

- **Source:** [UC Riverside](https://news.ucr.edu/articles/2026/07/24/new-tool-identifies-sources-fake-videos)
- **Published:** 2026-07-24
- **Why it matters:** Identifying which system produced a synthetic video could support forensic investigations and accountability beyond a simple real-or-fake classification.

A UC Riverside-led team developed SAGA, a system that analyses characteristic visual patterns to attribute generated videos to their source models. Researchers tested it on public datasets containing output from 19 text-to-video and image-to-video generators and report that it can distinguish real from synthetic video, identify generation mode, separate model versions and infer the development team behind a model. The results are researcher-reported, and the source article does not establish performance against future or deliberately adversarial generators.
