### Malaysia News

#### 1. Statistics Bill heads to Dewan Negara after lower-house approval

- **Source:** [Malay Mail, reporting Bernama](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/07/27/new-statistics-bill-aims-to-modernise-malaysias-data-system-says-akmal-nasrullah/229139)
- **Published:** 2026-07-27
- **Why it matters:** Replacing the 1965 law could strengthen the governance, independence and usefulness of Malaysia's official statistics while retaining confidentiality safeguards.

Economy Minister Akmal Nasrullah Mohd Nasir said the Statistics Bill 2026 had passed the Dewan Rakyat and was scheduled for the Dewan Negara next week, with gazettement targeted by year-end after the remaining administrative process. The bill would replace the Statistics Act 1965, establish a National Statistics Council to oversee statistical development and provide for a data-repository centre supporting broader analysis. Passage by the upper house and gazettement were still pending at publication time.

#### 2. AMRO raises Malaysia's 2026 growth forecast to 4.9%

- **Source:** [Bernama](https://asean.bernama.com/news.php?id=2586489)
- **Published:** 2026-07-27
- **Why it matters:** The upgrade links Malaysia's near-term growth directly to AI-related exports and data-centre investment, while highlighting exposure to external demand and policy risks.

The ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office raised its 2026 growth forecast for Malaysia from 4.6% to 4.9%, citing stronger-than-expected first-half exports and investment. AMRO expects continued strength in AI-driven exports, data-centre investment and liquefied-natural-gas production, but projects growth to ease to 4.7% in 2027 and expects some second-half moderation in domestic-oriented manufacturing. It maintained a 2.0% headline-inflation forecast and warned that West Asian risks, US monetary policy and US tariff policy could sustain short-term ringgit volatility.

#### 3. At least nine Rohingya schools close amid threats and online hate campaign

- **Source:** [Reuters via The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/asia/rohingya-schools-malaysia-face-closures-amid-online-surge-hate-speech-1498996?amp)
- **Published:** 2026-07-27
- **Why it matters:** School closures deprive refugee children of education and show how online misinformation can translate into offline intimidation of a vulnerable community.

Reuters reported that at least nine Rohingya refugee schools across Malaysia had closed because of safety fears or official action after harassment, threats and an online hate campaign. The report said a May wave of hostile content followed hygiene allegations tied to ritual cattle slaughter, while a petition calling for Rohingya removal drew nearly half a million signatures before being taken down in June. Malaysia does not formally recognise refugees, although about 120,000 Rohingya are registered with the UN refugee agency; the Home Ministry did not respond to Reuters' request for comment on the closures or hate speech.
