### Malaysia News

#### 1. Tabung Haji inquiry finds political interference and calls for forensic audits

- **Source:** [Malay Mail, reporting the Royal Commission of Inquiry and Bernama](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/07/30/tabung-haji-rci-report-reveals-political-interference-in-pilgrim-funds-management/229486); [Malay Mail, reporting the RCI and Bernama](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/07/30/rci-wants-forensic-audit-into-14-tabung-haji-investments/229485)
- **Published:** 2026-07-30
- **Why it matters:** The findings raise major questions about governance and depositor funds at Malaysia's Islamic pilgrimage savings institution and identify specific investments for further scrutiny.

The newly released RCI report found weaknesses including political interference in Tabung Haji's management and profit distribution and recommended forensic audits of 14 investment decisions made between 2014 and 2020. A related finding said full application of Malaysian Financial Reporting Standards would have turned Tabung Haji's reported RM3.4 billion profit for 2017 into a RM1.4 billion net loss. The commission also urged prompt action on alleged misconduct and said the government should provide an agreed RM1.73 billion annual allocation for early redemption of sukuk issued after assets were transferred to Urusharta Jamaah.

#### 2. Twelve detained over alleged MyIMMs hacking and immigration bribery scheme

- **Source:** [Malay Mail, reporting Sinar Harian and MACC](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/07/30/report-myimms-hacking-syndicate-allegedly-paid-up-to-rm6000-in-bribes-to-immigration-officers/229507)
- **Published:** 2026-07-30
- **Why it matters:** If substantiated, the case would expose both cybersecurity and insider-corruption weaknesses in a system controlling foreign-worker immigration approvals.

The Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission detained 12 people, including six Immigration Department officers and a police officer, over suspected hacking of the MyIMMs system to obtain temporary employment visit passes without proper procedures. An unnamed source cited by the report alleged that officers received RM1,000 to RM6,000 in bribes; MACC confirmed the arrests and said it was investigating under Sections 16(a) and 17(a) of the MACC Act 2009. The allegations have not been proven in court, and the report did not identify formal charges.

### AI News

#### 1. OpenAI offers advanced ChatGPT access to an initial 10,000 academic researchers

- **Source:** [SiliconANGLE, reporting OpenAI's programme announcement](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/29/openai-opens-new-chatgpt-academic-researchers-program-100000-scientists/)
- **Published:** 2026-07-29
- **Why it matters:** Broad free access to frontier models could accelerate research workflows while making independent evaluation of their scientific reliability increasingly important.

OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Academic Researchers as part of a US$250 million science initiative, initially offering free access to 10,000 researchers at selected universities and planning to add 90,000 more over time. The programme includes higher usage limits, larger context windows, expanded deep research, Codex, ChatGPT Work and OpenAI's latest models, alongside training and integration assistance. The expected research benefits are company claims, and the announcement does not provide independent evidence that the tools improve scientific accuracy.

#### 2. US plans US$300 million GlobalFoundries award for silicon photonics

- **Source:** [Reuters via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/us-to-award-globalfoundries-300-million-to-develop-faster-ai-chip-links/)
- **Published:** 2026-07-29
- **Why it matters:** Faster, lower-power optical links are increasingly important as data movement becomes a bottleneck in large AI systems, while the award also seeks to build more of that supply chain in the United States.

GlobalFoundries said the US Department of Commerce will award it US$300 million in CHIPS Act funding for research and development in silicon photonics and co-packaged optics for AI data centres. The company is targeting data-transfer speeds of 400 gigabits per second and energy efficiency up to five times greater than current implementations; the funding complements a US$1.5 billion award made in 2024 for factories in New York and Vermont. The performance targets are forward-looking company claims rather than independently verified deployments.
